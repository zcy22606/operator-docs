# Negotiating with sites

## Where to find sites

| Channel | How |
|---|---|
| People you already know | Start with owners you know. These close most easily |
| Trade clusters | Auto parts markets, repair streets, garment districts, restaurant rows. You can visit many in one trip |
| Trade associations, chambers of commerce | Reach a group of owners at once, with more trust built in |
| Referrals from current sites | Owners introducing each other have a high success rate |
| Suppliers and wholesalers | Parts, fabric and food suppliers know huge numbers of businesses |
| Local listings | Filter by category on maps and review platforms, then visit |

Screen first with the [gold standard](/en/before/scenes#gold-standard). If a site is weak on all three tests, skip it.

## The process

1. **First visit:** explain the project, look around, find out how many workers there are and what each one does
2. **First assessment:** check against the gold standard and see how many scenes the site has and how long it could run
3. **Trial:** for a new site type, record 2 hours with one device and wait for the review
4. **Sign:** once the trial passes, sign the [site agreement](/en/business/templates/store-agreement). Worker [consent forms](/en/business/templates/operator-consent) are optional
5. **Register:** send the site and worker details to your contact
6. **Deploy:** set up the devices as in [When the devices arrive](/en/after/receiving-devices)
7. **Close the round:** when the agreed period ends or the work is saturated, the devices move on. Close well, so you can work together again and ask the owner for introductions

## Opening the conversation

Don't lead with AI and robots. Lead with what's in it for the owner and whether it affects the business. Something like:

> We're running a project where skilled workers wear a small camera and just do their normal work. It doesn't slow anything down. The video is used to teach robots how skilled work is done. The shop and the worker wearing the camera both get paid. Let's try two hours and see if it fits.

Make three things clear:

- **It doesn't get in the way:** workers carry on as normal, no stopping to explain, no performing
- **It pays:** the shop and the workers both get paid, and you explain how in person
- **It has limits:** only work is recorded; toilets, changing rooms and other private areas are off limits

## Questions owners ask

| Question | How to answer |
|---|---|
| What is this for? | Training robots and AI to learn hand skills. It records how hands and tools move, not people |
| Will my shop's information get out? | Data is uploaded encrypted, used only for model training, and never published. Payment QR codes, phone numbers and payment details are avoided. It's all written into the agreement |
| Will it affect business? | The device is light and worn on the head; workers carry on as normal without stopping |
| Will my staff agree? | Taking part is voluntary. Anyone who doesn't want to wear it doesn't, and they can stop at any time |
| How much do I get? | Explain the pay arrangement you've chosen and put it in the agreement |
| How long does it run? | It's agreed as a round, usually a few weeks. Devices rotate between roles and workers during the round, then move on |
| Who pays if a device breaks? | Normal wear isn't the shop's responsibility. Details are in the agreement |
| What do I need to do? | Provide the shop's details, decide who wears the device, and give the devices a place to charge and be stored |

## Negotiation points

### Agree a round, not a day rate

Agree each site as one round: roughly how many weeks, which roles, which workers take turns. The owner knows what to expect, and you can plan rotation.

### Decide the pay arrangement beforehand

The three common options are a share of accepted hours, fixed pay per recording hour or day, and one fee per round. See [Deciding site and worker pay](/en/before/payment#site-pay).

- To get the site invested in quality, use a share
- If the owner wants something simple and fixed, use fixed pay or a one-off fee

### Explain rotation up front

Say clearly at the start: once a scene has enough data, the device moves to other roles or workers, and after the round it moves to another site. If you skip this, moving devices later causes friction.

### Don't promise what you can't deliver

- Don't promise a specific number of hours or a specific amount of money; the review decides
- Don't promise a renewal
- Don't name specific clients or uses beyond what the agreement says
- Don't agree to the site changing device settings, recording selectively or staging work

## Before deploying, have these in hand

- A signed site agreement
- Site and worker details. See [Before the devices arrive](/en/after/before-devices)

## Keeping the relationship strong

- Check in with the owner once a week on progress and any problems
- Pay on time, as agreed
- Deal with workers' concerns quickly
- Close each round properly, and ask whether the owner would introduce other shops
