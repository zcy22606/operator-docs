# Day-to-day operations

## The daily rhythm

| When | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| Before work | Operator or the site's designated person | Power on, connect the phone to set the clock, check settings, scan the code, put the device on |
| During work | Worker | Keep recording and work as usual |
| End of day | Upload lead | Stop recording, swap cards, copy, start the upload |
| Next morning | Upload lead | Confirm the upload was received, then clear the copies and the SD card |
| Every day | Operator | Read the review results, fix problems, arrange rotations |

## How to record

Keep recording for the whole working period. Review picks out skill segments of 30 seconds or more, built around one outcome. Simple work in between isn't paid, and isn't penalized either.

- **Start recording when work starts** and work normally. **Don't switch on and off for each step**, and don't pick and choose what to record
- **Switch off only in three cases:** a break longer than a minute; no work to do; going to a toilet, changing room or other private area
- **Stop recording only when changing worker or site**, and scan the new code when you do. The order is: stop recording → scan the new code → start recording

## Basic rules (every recording)

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Clothing | Everyone in frame at minimum: men in shorts or longer; women with tops covering chest and midriff, plus shorts, trousers or a skirt. If anyone isn't, stop recording at once. **Three detected nudity incidents end the partnership** |
| Real work | Work that would be done anyway and that people earn a living from. Nothing arranged or acted |
| Visible progress | By the end of a segment, the thing in hand has changed: cut, joined, fixed, shaped. Don't redo finished work |
| Normal pace | No slowing down, no exaggerated movements |
| No idling | No staring into space, scrolling a phone or pretending to work. Natural pauses such as waiting for glue to dry, checking results or thinking about the next step are fine |
| Hands in frame | Hands, tool tips and the workpiece in view and clear almost the whole time. Segments with hands out of frame for 10 seconds or more are removed |
| Self-worn | Head-mounted stereo camera pointing down along the natural line of sight. No tripods, no one else holding it |
| Privacy | Avoid payment QR codes, phone numbers and payment details where possible |

## What work counts {#what-counts}

### Basic skills

Real hand skill, where a slip would spoil the work. Each segment shows at least one of these:

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Precision control | Holding angle, line, force, tension or position | Soldering to a small contact, sewing a curved seam, stringing a racket, shaving a hairline |
| Judgment | Reading a signal, measurement or defect and deciding the next step | Testing a board then choosing which part to replace; inspecting a weld then reworking it |
| Fit and adjust | Adjusting interdependent parts until they fit and work | Fitting brake shoes and springs; measuring, cutting and fitting pipe |
| Process control | Adjusting as temperature, time or material changes, and recovering when things go wrong | Welding, wok cooking, rethreading after a yarn break |

Repeating one skilled action is fine (stitch after stitch) as long as each one needs care. Staying at one workstation for the whole segment is also fine.

### Advanced work

A complete job built on basic skills, meeting all seven of these:

1. At least 3 distinct steps in a meaningful order (measure → cut → fit → check)
2. Uses more than one skill
3. Not one action running through the whole segment
4. Moves around the work area or moves the workpiece because the job requires it
5. At a business, not at home (home workshops excepted)
6. At least one inspection, measurement or test, followed by acting on the result
7. Standard equipment and standard settings

The same kind of work can fall into either tier. Soldering wire after wire at a bench is a basic skill. Finding a board fault, replacing the part and testing the fix is advanced work.

For what doesn't count, see [These aren't skills either](/en/before/scenes#not-skills).

## Briefing workers

Before someone wears a device for the first time, spend 15 minutes on these points:

1. **Why we record:** to teach robots how to do the work, so it has to be real work at normal speed
2. **How to wear it:** on the head, lens pointing down along the line of sight, hands and workpiece in frame
3. **When to switch off:** breaks over a minute, no work, private areas
4. **Clothing:** everyone in frame must be properly dressed; stop immediately if not
5. **No performing:** work as usual, no slowing down, no narrating

Spend extra time on site in the first two days. If hands keep leaving the frame or the angle is off, correct it on the spot.

## Quotas and rotation {#rotation}

Each buyer will only take so much data from any one scene. Once that's full, further recording doesn't count as accepted hours. Simpler scenes fill up faster. So you need as many scenes as possible, with devices rotating and spreading across them.

- GI Labs tracks quotas centrally. Your contact will tell you when a scene is full or a site needs to change
- When you get a notice, act quickly: move the device to another role, or to the next scene or the next site
- Scan the new code after every change
- Make it clear to sites from the start that devices rotate between roles and workers, and move on once the round is done

**Have the next scene ready in advance.** Don't wait for a notice to start looking. Every idle day is a day of lost income.

## Daily checklist

### Start of day (every device)

- [ ] Card in, then power on; power bank fully charged
- [ ] Connect the phone once to set the clock. A device that hasn't been connected records dates as 1970
- [ ] Device page shows idle, enough free space, latest firmware
- [ ] Resolution 1728×1200, hand detection on (check again after any firmware upgrade)
- [ ] Scanned the right site × worker code for today
- [ ] Angle adjusted, preview closed, recording started

Open the live preview only to adjust the angle, then close it.

### End of day (upload lead)

- [ ] Stop recording, take out the card, put in the other card
- [ ] Copy each card to the computer or external drive and check it's complete
- [ ] Start the upload; it can run overnight

### Next morning (upload lead)

- [ ] Confirm the upload was received
- [ ] Clear the copies on the computer and the SD card
- [ ] Put the card back in the device

### Every day (operator)

- [ ] Read the day's review results and act on the feedback
- [ ] Arrange scene or device changes as notified
- [ ] Collect details for new sites and workers and send them to your contact
