# Which sites qualify

Only go to businesses where people do real skilled work. Whether a scene works depends on what the workers do with their hands, not on what kind of shop it is.

A **scene** here means one kind of work setting and work content, such as auto repair, phone repair, tailoring, a restaurant kitchen or machine setup. Data volume, quotas and how much you need to prepare are all counted by scene.

## The gold standard: three tests {#gold-standard}

The more a job meets these, the better.

| Test | How to judge |
|---|---|
| Skill | The longer a newcomer needs to learn it, the better |
| Steps | The more steps one piece of work involves, the better |
| Tools and conditions | The more tools it uses and the more each job varies, the better |

When unsure about a scene, run it through these three. If it's weak on all three, don't pursue it.

### Good vs bad

| Test | Good: repair (auto, appliance, phone) | Bad: driving screws on an electronics line |
|---|---|---|
| Skill | Years before you can diagnose faults alone | Learned in half a day |
| Steps | Inspect, diagnose, disassemble, replace, reassemble, test | Pick part, align, tighten, repeat |
| Tools and conditions | Multimeter, wrenches, soldering iron in turn; every machine has a different fault | One power screwdriver; every part identical |

## Where to look

These directions usually meet the gold standard. Whether a scene actually works is decided by the trial recording.

| Direction | Examples | Roles to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Repair | Auto repair, appliance repair, phone repair, bike and e-bike repair, watch repair | Mechanics, technicians |
| Making and fabrication | Tailoring, alterations, leatherwork, shoe repair, carpentry, metalwork, welding | The craftspeople doing the work |
| Commercial kitchens | Restaurant kitchens, noodle and dumpling makers, bakeries | Cooks, pastry and bread makers |
| Personal services | Hair salons, nail salons, optical shops | Stylists, nail technicians, opticians |
| Construction and renovation | Electrical, plumbing, tiling, joinery, painting | Tradespeople |
| Factories | Plants with machinery | Machine setup, equipment repair, rework, quality inspection. **Not line stations** |

## What doesn't qualify

- **Simple repetitive work:** shelving, restocking, sorting, carrying, packing, labeling, sales floor work
- **Household-type work:** cleaning, wiping, laundry, folding, tidying, even when done at a business
- **Repetitive steps on a production line:** almost never counts

### These aren't skills either {#not-skills}

| Case | Why it doesn't count |
|---|---|
| A welder sweeping the floor | It's the work that matters, not the person's title. Sweeping is sweeping |
| Sorting small screws | Small doesn't mean hard. It's still sorting |
| Carrying a hot pan | Dangerous doesn't mean skilled. It's still carrying |
| Stacking boxes for two hours | Long and tiring is still stacking boxes |
| Opening parcels with five tools | More tools and steps, still opening parcels |
| Five seconds of soldering in the middle of packing | Too short. Under 30 seconds doesn't count |

## Requirements for the site itself

- **It must be a real business, not someone's home.** A home workshop that someone makes a living from is fine, and so is outdoor work
- **Workers must be people who actually do this job.** No one brought in to act
- **Sites with more workers and more kinds of skilled work last longer**
- **More scenes is worth more than recording one scene for longer**

### Each site has an hours cap {#site-cap}

The cap depends on the size of the business:

| Size | Staff | Cap per site |
|---|---|---|
| Small | Under 15 | 500 hours |
| Medium | 15–100 | 2,000 hours |
| Large | Over 100 | 5,000 hours |

Medium and large sites must provide business registration details.

## Trial new scenes first {#trial}

1. **A scene we already collect:** start as soon as it's signed
2. **A new scene:** record 2 hours with one device, upload, and wait for the review
3. **Trial passed:** roll out fully

If the trial fails, your contact will tell you why. Sometimes a different role at the same site will pass.

## How many scenes you need {#how-many}

Each buyer will only take so much data from any one scene. Simpler scenes fill up faster, and once a scene is full, more recording doesn't count. The more scenes you have, the more easily devices keep rotating instead of sitting idle.

### How to estimate

```
Scenes needed per month ≈ total device recording hours per month ÷ hours one scene can take
```

- **Total device recording hours**: each device usually records 30–45 hours a week; multiply by the number of devices
- **Hours one scene can take**: set by buyer demand, and it varies a lot. Complex scenes can take much more; simple ones fill quickly. Ask your contact for the current quota for each kind of scene

### In practice

- **Prioritize complex scenes**, which last longer; use simpler ones as fill-ins in rotation
- **One site can have several scenes**: a general repair shop might do phone, appliance and e-bike repair
- **Each branch of a chain** is a separate site you can roll out to one by one
- **Keep opening new scenes.** Don't wait for a saturation notice to start looking
